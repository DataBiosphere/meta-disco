The Missing Participant Key

The open corpus is 733,877 files and roughly four thousand named subjects. AnVIL models those subjects completely and populates them almost not at all. Three use cases for participant-level search, the metadata each needs, and the single change that unblocks the first one.

3,212
1000 Genomes participants underpin 679,433 files — 92.6% of the AnVIL open-access corpus. 3,210 of them appear in more than one dataset. Four more cohorts account for most of the rest.
01

Why this is tractable

Asked what they want from a search system, scientists say “everything.” The corpus is 733,877 files, which makes everything sound enormous. Viewed by participant it is not: the open-access corpus resolves to roughly three thousand people, and one public registry — the 1000 Genomes sample panel and pedigree — covers nearly all of them.

The 3,202 figure matches the Byrska-Bishop expanded 1000G cohort exactly, including its 602 trios. Population, sex, and family structure for these participants are already public.

The GTEx result is the useful one. /api/v2/dataset/sample returns tissueSiteDetail: "Whole Blood" with uberonId: "UBERON:0013756" — an ontology-coded anatomical site, for exactly the field AnVIL reports as null on every row. Forty-seven samples for one donor.

There is also a third crosswalk already in production. T2T's FASTQ files are named by ENA run accession — 3,202 of them in ANVIL_T2T, exactly one per participant, plus 279 more in ANVIL_T2T_CHRY. validate_ena_accessions.py already parses these from the file name and resolved 6,830 of 6,962 FASTQ records against ENA on the last run, with zero platform mismatches. ENA therefore reaches the same people by a different key than the sample ID does.

One honest negative: searching ENCODE for our ENCORE target genes — RNF26, PRPF40A, ZC3H7A — returns zero each. ENCODE is the natural home for that data but does not appear to hold it yet. No BioSample, BioProject, GEO or phs accession appears in any file name anywhere in the corpus.

02

Three use cases

Deliberately a ladder. Each rung needs strictly more metadata and more governance than the one below it, so the set can be adopted in order — and the third can be deferred without abandoning the idea.

UC 1

Matched-technology cohort assembly

“Every participant who has both an HPRC de novo assembly and CHM13-aligned short-read CRAMs — give me the matched pairs.”

Benchmarking assembly-based against read-based variant calling. The answer exists in data already on disk and cannot be asked for in the Explorer today.

  • participant identifier
  • data_type
  • reference_assembly

No participant attributes required · no governance change

UC 2

Ancestry- and family-stratified selection

“Thirty participants from African-ancestry populations with long-read data, including complete trios.”

Pangenome expansion, population-specific reference panels, imputation QC — the reason HPRC sampled for diversity in the first place.

  • participant identifier
  • data_type
  • reference_assembly
  • population / ancestry
  • sex
  • family & relationship
  • assay_type

Attributes are public and free · requires a crosswalk to the 1000G panel

UC 3

Phenotype-driven case finding

“Unsolved rare-disease participants with a given HPO phenotype who have both WGS and RNA-seq, in families with an affected sibling.”

The query rare-disease researchers actually want, and the one that justifies participant-level search existing at all. GREGoR is the natural partner.

  • participant identifier
  • population / sex / family
  • assay_type
  • HPO phenotype
  • affected / solved status
  • consent enforcement on results

Controlled access · for rare phenotypes even a result count can disclose

03

The model is complete. The data is not.

AnVIL's TDR schema (version 5) defines twelve tables and seventeen relationships. anvil_donor carries sex, reported ethnicity and genetic ancestry; anvil_biosample carries anatomical site, type and age at collection; anvil_diagnosis carries disease, phenotype and a phenopacket field. Every attribute the three use cases need already has a slot.

Azul indexes all of it — /index/donors, /index/biosamples, /index/activities are first-class endpoints, and a compact manifest exposes sixty columns per file including donors.donor_id.

Here is what those columns actually contain for ANVIL_1000G_high_coverage_2019, a fully indexed dataset of 26,016 files:

Share of 26,016 manifest rows with a non-empty value. Identity is wired; content is absent.

The one populated attribute anywhere in the probe is activities.activity_type, and every value reads Checksum || Indexing || Unknown — provenance bookkeeping, not sequencing. The anvil_sequencingactivity and anvil_alignmentactivity tables, where assay_type and reference_assembly would live, contribute nothing.

04

The identifier is minted per snapshot

Grouping files by participant works inside one dataset. Across datasets it fails completely, and not because the people differ.

1,285 individuals are demonstrably present in both ANVIL_1000G_high_coverage_2019 and ANVIL_T2T, identified by the same sample ID in their file names. Every one of them carries a different donor_id in each dataset. The intersection of the two donor sets is zero.

05

The join recovers metadata AnVIL already holds

This is the argument that does not depend on anyone curating anything new.

Column ANVIL_1000G_high_coverage_2019 ANVIL_T2T
donors indexed 3,202 3,202
donors.phenotypic_sex populated 0 / 3,202 3,202 / 3,202

The same 3,202 people. Sex is recorded for every one of them in one dataset and for none of them in the other — 1,603 female, 1,599 male, sitting in T2T while 1000G reports null across the board. A participant registry does not only enable new queries; it recovers metadata that is already present but stranded per snapshot.

Both datasets are fully indexed as of 2026-08-23, so these are final counts rather than a floor. The identity of the 3,202 is established by file name: the two datasets' sample-ID sets intersect exactly.

The ask

A stable cross-snapshot participant identifier.

Not new modelling, not a schema extension, not “populate everything.” One durable key. UC 1 follows entirely from it, and the sex example above shows the payoff before any new curation happens.

06

What we can build without waiting

UC 1 is reachable today. Pull a compact manifest per dataset, join donors.donor_id to the sample ID parsed from the file name, and union across datasets. The manifest is a richer ingest path than the /index/files endpoint we currently use — it carries donor, biosample, diagnosis and activity identity that the JSON response strips.

Two things must go to AnVIL rather than being solved locally: the stable identifier above, and populating the donor, biosample and activity attributes that the schema already defines. For UC 3, a data-sharing conversation with GREGoR precedes both.

Snapshot caveat

The anvil15 catalog is mid-reindex. It reached 507,362 files across 9 datasets on 23 August, up from 200,454 across 4 the day before. ANVIL_T2T and ANVIL_1000G_high_coverage_2019 are now complete, so the figures above are final for those two. ANVIL_T2T_CHRY is about half done and ANVIL_HPRC has not started, so any count involving them is still a floor — including the 249-participant HPRC ∩ T2T figure that motivates UC 1.

Attribute population is inching up rather than arriving: across 8,669 donors, sex is now set on 3,364 and reported ethnicity on exactly three. Diagnoses and activity assay type remain wholly empty.

How each figure was obtained

Participant counts and cross-dataset overlap
Regex extraction of NA#####/HG##### over the 733,877-record corpus snapshot downloaded 2026-07-29 from the Azul files index.
Subject inventory across all cohorts
The same snapshot scanned for thirteen accession grammars — 1000G/Coriell, GTEx donor and sample, NHP dGTEx donor, ENCORE target, ONT flow cell, IGVF, ENA/SRA run, experiment, sample and study, BioSample, BioProject, GEO and phs. Boundary assertions matter here: a trailing \b silently misses an accession followed by an underscore, which is how the 6,962 ENA-named FASTQs were missed on the first pass.
External registries
GTEx and ENCODE probed live on 2026-08-22 (gtexportal.org/api/v2, encodeproject.org/search). ENA figures read from output/anvil/ena_validation_results.json, the stored result of the last validate_ena_accessions.py run. The 1000G panel at internationalgenome.org was not probed this round — that row rests on the study dossiers.
Azul entity endpoints and facets
Live probe of service.explore.anvilproject.org on 2026-08-22: /index/donors, /index/biosamples, /index/activities, and the facet list returned with /index/files.
Manifest columns and population rates
Compact manifests requested with PUT /fetch/manifest/files filtered to one dataset, polled to completion and downloaded in full: 26,016 rows for 1000G, 96,970 for T2T. Column population counted directly over the TSV.
Donor identity behaviour
Set intersection of the two manifests' donors.donor_id values, cross-referenced against sample IDs parsed from files.file_name in the same rows.
TDR schema
Read from src/azul/plugins/metadata/anvil/schema.py in DataBiosphere/azul, which vendors the AnVIL table definitions (version 5, 12 tables, 17 relationships). Snapshot names and BigQuery projects read from sources[].source_spec on Azul dataset records. Table contents were not read — that needs Terra credentials.
